# Connections

Connections can transfer energy between two nodes. Parameters for the connection are defined in the `connection` entity, but the two `nodes` it connects are defined by establishing an entity between `connection--leftNode--rightNode`.

## Defining how the connection functions

- `transfer_method` to define the way the connection transfers energy between the nodes: *regular* (default), *exact*, *variable_cost_only*, *no_losses_no_variable_cost* or *unidirectional* (see [the basics](../how_to/basics.md)). A *no_losses_no_variable_cost* connection (and a line of a `dc_power_flow_with_angles` group, which uses it internally) carries both directions in one signed flow, so it cannot have different values in the two directions:
    - it cannot model losses (a loss one way would be a gain the other way), so its `efficiency` is ignored (treated as 1, with a warning: W-0591) when the two nodes have the same `unit_of_measure` or either has none, and always on a line of a `dc_power_flow_with_angles` group. For losses, use *regular* or *exact*.
    - between nodes with different `unit_of_measure` values, the `efficiency` is a conversion between the units (e.g. MWh to t), the same in both directions: the flow f arriving at the second node takes f / `efficiency` from the first, and in the reverse direction the first node gets f / `efficiency` for the f taken from the second. FlexTool warns about it (W-0589).
    - its `other_operational_cost` is ignored (it would be a cost one way and a revenue the other), with a warning (W-0590). For a variable cost, use *variable_cost_only*, *regular* or *exact*.
- `startup_method` gives the connection online capacity. *linear*: the online capacity is anything between 0 and the built capacity; *integer*: whole units of the connection (of `virtual_unitsize`, else the whole connection), computationally more demanding; *linear_fix_in_uc* and *integer_fix_in_uc*: the same, and in a separate realization solve the online capacity over the jump (the realized periods) is kept at what the stochastic solve chose. The connection's flow in each direction is limited by its online capacity × availability, its reserve by its online capacity, and `startup_cost` is paid per MW brought online. Connections have no minimum load or minimum up/down times. Not available for connections in a `dc_power_flow_with_angles` group, other than `is_DC` connections (an error: switching a DC power-flow line off would need line-switching constraints).
- `invest_method` to define investment and retirement limits: either *not_allowed* or then a combination of 
    - *invest* and/or *retire* 
    - investment limits for each *period* and/or for all periods (*total*) or *no_limit* 
    - *cumulative_limits* considers investments, retirements and existing together, requires `cumulative_max_capacity` and/or `cumulative_min_capacity`
    - any method other than *not_allowed* offers investment (retirement) in every investment period, also without `invest_cost` (`net_salvage_value`) or where it is 0: that capacity has no annuity, its `fixed_cost` still counts. To forbid investment in a period, use a *period* method (*invest_period*, *invest_period_total* and their *invest_retire_* forms) and set `invest_max_period` 0 for it: the other methods do not use `invest_max_period`. A 0 in an `invest_cost` map does not forbid it.
- `lifetime_method` - What happens when capacity reaches the end of its `lifetime_technical`, as for units: *reinvest_automatic* (default) replaces it, without a model choice, by a new plant at the values of the period the replacement comes online, to the end of the horizon; *reinvest_choice* retires it and the model decides how much new capacity to build; *no_reinvestment* retires it and allows investment only during the first technical lifetime from the model start. See [lifetime, age and end of life](units.md#lifetime-age-and-end-of-life).

## Main data items for connections

- `existing` - [MW or count] Existing capacity or number of units. If `virtual_unitsize` is given, then `existing` is the number of units (capacity = `existing` × `virtual_unitsize`). Otherwise `existing` is the existing capacity of a single unit (typically in MW). Constant or period.
- `efficiency` - [factor, typically between 0-1] Efficiency of a connection. Constant, period, time or period+time. On a *no_losses_no_variable_cost* connection it is ignored, or a unit conversion that applies in both directions between nodes in different units (see `transfer_method`).
- `delay` - [hours] Not yet applied to connections (planned). A connection `delay` is ignored: the connection is modelled exactly as if no delay were set (the flow arrives in the same time step) and the model prints a warning naming the connection. To model a delayed flow between two nodes, use a unit with `delay` instead.
- `startup_cost` - [CUR/MW] Cost of starting up one MW of capacity (used when `startup_method` activates online variables on the connection). Constant or period.
- `reactance` - [p.u.] Per-unit reactance of the transmission line. Used for DC power flow when the enclosing `group__node` has `transfer_method = dc_power_flow_with_angles`.
- `constraint_invested_capacity_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient) putting the capacity that comes online in each period on the left side of the user-defined constraint. Without a construction lead time, this is the capacity invested in that period. Coefficient per MW. Renamed from `constraint_capacity_coefficient`; the old expression summed `v_invest` once per active investment period, giving incorrect results in multi-period models. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `constraint_cumulative_pre_built_capacity_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient). Puts the capacity in service at the start of each period on the left side of the user constraint. This is existing capacity plus capacity that came online in earlier periods, minus earlier retirements. Capacity coming online in the period itself is in the invested term. Enables learning-effect and period-over-period growth limits (pair with `constraint_invested_capacity_coeff` on the same constraint). Coefficient per MW. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `constraint_capacity_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient). Puts the connection's capacity in service in each period on the left side of the user constraint (existing plus investments still in service minus retirements). Coefficient per MW. Capacity in construction is not included. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `constraint_retired_capacity_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient). Puts the capacity retired in each period on the left side of the user constraint. Coefficient per MW. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `constraint_in_construction_capacity_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient). Puts the capacity in construction in each period on the left side of the user constraint. Capacity is in construction from the period of the investment decision until it comes online. It is zero without a construction lead time. Coefficient per MW. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `constraint_online_coeff` - [unit of the constraint per unit online] A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient). Puts the number of units of the connection online in each time step (both directions together) on the left side of the user constraint. One unit is `virtual_unitsize`; without it, the largest `existing` capacity, else 1000 MW — set `virtual_unitsize` on a connection that is invested in. Needs a `startup_method` other than *no_startup*; a coefficient on a connection without it is an error. With *linear* or *linear_fix_in_uc* the number is continuous. For MW online, use the unit size as the coefficient. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md).
- `other_operational_cost` - [CUR/MWh] Other operational variable cost for trasferring over the connection. Constant, period, time or period+time. Ignored on a *no_losses_no_variable_cost* connection (see `transfer_method`).
- `fixed_cost` - [CUR/kW/a] Annual fixed cost. New capacity pays it only while it operates. Constant or period.
- `invest_cost` - [CUR/kW] Overnight investment cost of new capacity. It is annualized over `payback_time` with `discount_rate`; with `lifetime_method` *reinvest_choice* or *no_reinvestment* that annuity is spread evenly over `lifetime_technical` and paid for each year the capacity is in service (with *reinvest_automatic*: each replacement at the `invest_cost` of the period it comes online, for its years in service). Construction time costs with their interest must be included here. Constant or period: the value of the period when the capacity is built (comes online) is used.
- `discount_rate` - [e.g. 0.05 equals 5%] Discount rate for investments (WACC). Reflects the financing cost and risk for this technology. In the same terms as the cost inputs: a real rate for inputs in real prices, a nominal rate for nominal inputs. Not the model's `social_discount_rate`, which weighs costs over time. Constant or period.
- `lifetime_technical` - [years] Technical lifetime: how long the capacity operates, from its start of operation (existing capacity: from the model start minus its age). Its end is rounded to a period start by `end_of_life_method`. Without `payback_time`, `invest_cost` is annualized over it. Constant or period: the value of the period the capacity comes online.
- `payback_time` - [years] Years over which `invest_cost` is annualized with `discount_rate`; it sets only the size of the level yearly price the capacity pays over its `lifetime_technical` (see [discount calculations](units.md#discount-calculations)). No value: `lifetime_technical`. Constant or period.
- `age_at_model_start` - [years] or `build_year` - [year] (with the `model` parameter `start_year`): the age of the existing capacity at the model start, which shortens its remaining life. Give one, not both. Constant.
- `end_of_life_method` - How an end of life inside a period is rounded to a period start: *closest_seam* (default), *previous_seam* (for capacity-adequacy studies) or *next_seam*, as for units.
- `construction_lead_time` - [years] Time from the investment decision until the capacity starts to operate. The start is rounded to the start of a period (see `construction_lead_time_method`). Investments whose rounded start is after the end of the solve or the model horizon are not offered, with a warning. Constant or period. Default 0. See [construction lead time](../how_to/investment.md#how-to-make-investments-storageunit).
- `construction_lead_time_method` - How the start of operation (decision year plus `construction_lead_time`) is rounded to the start of a period: *immediate* (no lead time, the capacity operates from the decision period), *closest_seam* (the nearest period start; a tie goes to the later one), *previous_seam* (the period start before), *next_seam* (the period start after). Without a value: *immediate* when there is no lead time, else *closest_seam*.
- `life_extension_cost` - [CUR/kW] and `life_extension_years` - [years]: capacity reaching the end of its `lifetime_technical` can be kept in service `life_extension_years` longer for a one-off `life_extension_cost`, as for units, with `lifetime_method` *reinvest_choice* or *no_reinvestment* (see [life extension](units.md#life-extension)). Give both or neither. Constant or period: the value of the period the extension starts in.
- other investment parameters: `invest_max_total`, `invest_max_period`, `invest_min_total`, `invest_min_period`, `retire_max_total`, `retire_max_period`, `retire_min_total`, `retire_min_period`, `net_salvage_value` (an income received in full when capacity is retired, and a level yearly credit in the yearly price of capacity kept to its end of life; `existing` capacity can be retired with an `invest_method` that allows retirement, and with *invest_retire_…* or *cumulative_limits* also the capacity the model builds; see [retirements](units.md#retirements)). As for units, `inf` in `invest_max_period`, `retire_max_period` or `cumulative_max_capacity` means no limit in that period, and 0 or `-inf` in a minimum no minimum.
- `is_DC` - A flag whether the connection is DC: its flow into a node of a group with a *non_synchronous_limit* counts as non-synchronous (both directions; between two nodes of the same group it does not count). Default false. An `is_DC` connection inside a `dc_power_flow_with_angles` group is not a DC power-flow line: it keeps its own `transfer_method` and needs no `reactance`.
- `virtual_unitsize` - [MW] Size of one unit of the connection. With it, `existing` is the number of units. Integer start-ups (*integer*, *integer_fix_in_uc*) commit whole units of this size. Investments and retirements are continuous; their limits are in MW. No value: the existing capacity, or 1000 MW without it.
- `availability` - [e.g. 0.9 means 90%] Fraction of capacity available for connection flows. Constant, period, time or period+time.

## connection__profile

- `profile_method` - limits the connection's flow with a profile (*upper_limit*, *lower_limit*, *fixed*; the profile is a share of the capacity). The default is *none*: the profile is not applied, and FlexTool warns about a link without a `profile_method`. An *upper_limit* or *fixed* link to a profile without a value in the scenario stops the run with an error (it would hold the flow at 0). See [units constrained by profiles](units.md#units-constrained-by-profiles).

## connection__node (per-endpoint properties)

On the membership entity between a `connection` and one of its endpoint `node`s:

- `constraint_flow_coeff` - A map of coefficients (Index: constraint name, value: coefficient) placing this connection-to-node flow variable on the left side of a user-defined `constraint`. Applied per timestep. The flow counts in both directions (coefficient × (forward + reverse)), for every `transfer_method`. See [User constraints](user_constraints.md#the-coefficient-parameters).
