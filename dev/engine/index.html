    The engine pipeline

    flextool/engine/ is FlexTool's in-memory optimization engine. It reads a Spine input DB (or an in-memory test fixture) into one typed dataclass, derives the per-solve sets and parameters, builds an LP feature by feature on the polar_high eDSL, solves it with HiGHS via highspy and hands the solution to the next solve of the cascade and to process_outputs. The folder map is flextool/engine/README.md; the whole-system picture and the cross-cutting invariants are in architecture.md.

    Two repositories

    polar_high is the standalone LP eDSL — sets, parameters, variables, lazy constraint composition, warm-LP machinery, the autoscale range readout and Layer 3, and the Benders cut / recourse primitives. It knows nothing of FlexTool. engine is the FlexTool-side glue: the shape of FlexTool's data and model.

    The steps, with their modules, are listed in flextool/engine/README.md ("Pipeline"). Side-paths off this trunk:

    • Rolling-horizon / nested: every iteration loops back through overlay carrying a SolveHandoff. An identical next sub-solve re-solves the live WarmProblem; any other is built cold (Warm-start cascade).
    • Stochastic branches: solve/_stochastic.py decorates per-solve active-time lists with branch-suffixed periods before overlay sees them (Stochastic branches).
    • Spatial Benders: solve/_benders.solve_benders (Spatial Benders).

    Solve-level detail reduction

    solve.reduce_unit_detail_method / reduce_system_detail_method (user docs: entities_and_time.md "Leaving out detail in a solve") are applied in one place: preprocess._reduce_detail.apply_reduce_detail(d, unit, system), called by the cascade right after load_flextool for every sub-solve (_orchestration._drive_cascade). Every consumer after it reads the reduced data: the online / ramp carry check (check_online_ramp_carry, outside load_flextool so that it sees the reduced online set), the realization checks, the Benders split, the cold and warm build, the handoff and the per-roll outputs. The per-roll emit and the input derivation are not changed. Which has_* flags each level turns off is listed under Feature flags.

    • Copy, not replace. The transform returns a copy.copy of the FlexData with the changed fields set, not dataclasses.replace.

      A shallow copy keeps the extra attributes that the cascade and the emit hang on a FlexData (the axis vocabulary, output_static_data, reduced_output_features); dataclasses.replace would rebuild the object from its fields only and drop them. Unchanged frames are shared. A level that changes nothing returns the same object, so the LP is byte-identical.

    • Only clears, prunes, relaxes or folds to full load. Each part sets its fields to None (the field lists RAMP_FIELDS, ONLINE_FIELDS, RESERVE_FIELDS, ... in _reduce_detail.py), except relax_integrality (integer online columns continuous), the full-load efficiency of min_load_efficiency and multi-line piecewise_linear units under online* (a constant_efficiency unit: p_slope + p_section / W, p_section = 0, W the weighted output one unit online gives at full output, _unit_widths; a multi-line unit leaves process_piecewise and its lines with p_slope = 1/η(1) (W = 1) or max_g(α_g·W + β_g) / W, _full_load_piecewise; the full model's fuel at full output and its input limits; module docstring "Full-load efficiency"; under *_min_limits the same fold for the min_load_efficiency units with a negative p_section, _falling_min_load_eff, which also leave process_min_load_eff) and the pruning of online variables without a use.

      Under the integers* levels, online_uses lists, on the data after the level's removals, the uses of each process's online variables (ONLINE_USES: start-up cost, min load / capacity_min_coeff floors, min up / down times, the min_load_efficiency section, a written user constraint with constraint_online_coeff, a reserve, inertia, ramp limits, the parent's commitment pin; non-anticipativity ties are no use). The processes without a use lose their online variables: _prune_online filters the rows of PRUNE_FIELDS (the online sets and their parameters, the carry and pin, the min-time sets, p_startup_cost) to the other processes, and clears dt_online_carry with the last carried commitment (ReduceReport.removed["online_unused"]). The reserve use is conservative: maxFlow and the connection reserve rows hold the same reserve terms without the units online, so a reserve-only unit could be pruned too; it keeps its online variables, as the user docs say. test_reduce_detail_online_uses.py classifies every model reader of the online sets / variables (a new reader must be added as a use or as no use) and checks that pruning gives the optimum of the same units authored as no_startup.

      test_reduce_detail.py and test_reduce_system_detail.py check that every FlexData field of a feature is in its list by name, and for the system features also by the columns the frames hold. A new field of a feature must be added there.

    • Warm start. relax_integrality is a FlexData field in _warm._STRUCTURAL_FIELDS, so a warm LP built under one integrality is never reused under the other.

      The level strings are not fields: two sub-solves whose reduced data are identical may share a warm LP.

    • Run log and run status. The cascade prints one info line per solve at its first roll (_reduce_detail.summary_line: what each level left out and the interaction notes, ReduceReport.notes), logs the dropped user constraints as warnings, and records reduced = {unit, system, summary} on the run-status entry of every roll (RunRecorder.solve_reduced); summary is the line on the solve's first roll and None on the later ones.

    • Outputs. The static sets of the last sub-solve come from its pre-transform data (output_static_view), so a reduced last solve keeps the tables of earlier full solves.

      The cells of a left-out feature in a reduced solve's steps are emptied after the tables are built (process_outputs.reduced_cells): the cascade sets reduced_output_features on the reduced data, the per-roll writer writes it as output_raw/reduced_detail__<roll>.parquet, and write_outputs maps the markers onto the realised steps and periods (from the solve-keyed sets before drop_levels) and empties the rows of the feature tables (FEATURE_TABLES). Unreduced solves write no marker, so their tables are unchanged.

      A whole feature is one whose OUTPUT_FEATURES fields the transform cleared; online is keyed on the online sets alone (process_online*), since the carry and pin fields lose all their rows when every carried process is pruned while others keep their online variables. Online variables pruned for some processes only are per-entity markers (output_entities → reduced_output_entities): the marker's entity column holds the process (null for a whole feature), read_reduced_markers gives (feature, entity) keys, and mask_reduced_cells empties only that entity's column in the steps of the solves that pruned it. A period realised by both a keeping and a pruning solve keeps its average, with the pruning solve's steps in it as zeros (the same convention as whole features). The static online sets of the outputs keep every process that some solve so far kept (output_static_view(…, online_gone_everywhere), online_gone).

    • Carry across levels. The handoff records its producer's unit level (SolveHandoff.unit_detail) and the processes the producer left without online variables (SolveHandoff.online_left_out: all online processes under online*, those without a use under integers*).

      A solve that keeps online variables and continues the ramp flow of such a producer drops that flow for those processes only and starts their ramp and commitment cold (cold_start_online_flow_carry). Any other online process with a carried ramp flow but no online carry is a real inconsistency: check_online_ramp_carry raises for it.

    FlexData — the central data carrier

    FlexData (preprocess/flex_data.py) is a @dataclass of about 400 fields. Nine are positional and always present (the time / weighting / node floor); every other field defaults to None and is filled in only when the scenario exercises the corresponding feature.

    The naming convention is documented on the class:

    • Sets — index frames, pl.DataFrame, no prefix. Example: nodeBalance, process_source_sink, pss_dt, nodeState, cdt_eq, dtttdt.
    • Parameters — numeric polar_high.Param, p_* prefix. Example: p_inflow, p_unitsize, p_commodity_price, p_step_duration.
    • Variables created later by build_flextool are v_* for primal and vq_* for slack.

    The contract carried throughout the engine: a field is either populated with a frame / Param, or it is None. An empty frame is legal — it signals "this feature class is enabled, but no rows in this partition" (e.g. process_source_sink_noEff empty because every process is in the eff partition). None always means "loader didn't touch this field".

    How a DB-authored parameter is mapped onto the Param's axes (_param_shapes.py, PARAM_ALLOWED_SHAPES, resolve_param_shape) is in architecture.md Parameter shapes & (d,t) broadcasting. The resolver reads each Map level's index_name from the DB because a Map[time, period] and a Map[period, time] have the same polars column names but opposite axis semantics.

    Data sources

    The loaders are stacked on two protocols defined in input/_input_source.py:

    Source Implementation When used
    CsvSource _input_source.py Path wrapper for a sub-solve's work folder (input/ + solve_data/). load_flextool(path_or_csv_source, provider=..., db_reader=...) forms the provider keys from its directories and reads the provider, never the files.
    SpineDbReader _spinedb_reader.py The DB-direct reader — returns one polars frame per (entity_class, parameter_name) pair. Consumed by the override chain in _apply_db_overrides and by run_chain_from_db.
    InMemoryReader _inmemory_reader.py Same InputSource protocol as SpineDbReader, driven by an in-memory dict-of-frames fixture. The vehicle for fast unit tests under tests/engine/.

    FlexInputSource (work-folder shaped) and InputSource (per-parameter frame) are deliberately two protocols: the first names the sub-solve's directories (the paths the provider keys are formed from), the second requests one parameter at a time from the DB. load_flextool takes both: the work folder (source) and a required db_reader. The bulky pre-computed sets come from the sub-solve's FlexDataProvider, the parameters the override chain owns from the reader. The loader is provider-only: it never reads input/ or solve_data/ on disk, so a dump or stale CSVs in a reused work folder cannot change a run (Rule 4 of tests/engine/test_meta_provider_invariants.py). Only the unsupported flextool.engine._csv_reload.load_from_dump loads from CSVs (it seeds a provider from them first; architecture.md CSV dump).

    LP build: model/core.py and the feature modules

    build_flextool(m, d) builds the LP into the polar_high.Problem m from the FlexData d. Its body is a table of contents: a list of steps run in order, each taking the _Build context b (the problem, the data, the has_* feature flags, shared index sets, variables and a few shared expressions):

    1. _prepare — declares the dense axes, sets the has_* flags from the data, checks required fields and builds the shared index sets (pss_dt, nodeBalance_dt, nodeState_dt, …).
    2. _declare_variables — the one place where the decision space is assembled: core variables are declared here and the feature modules' add_variables(b) are called from here. Declaration order is the LP column order.
    3. _shared_expressions — expressions read by several families (reserve coupling sums, the capacity in service b.cap of _capacity, op_factor).
    4. One _eq_<family>(b) per core equation family, in emit order, interleaved with the feature modules' add_constraints(b).
    5. _obj_<family>(b) and the modules' objective_terms(b) return their term (or None); build_flextool sums them in order and calls m.set_objective.

    Every family function starts with an early-return guard on its flag (if not b.has_storage: return), so a feature whose data is empty emits nothing. The model/core.py module docstring is the map: every variable with its dims and unit, and every equation family in emit order with the module it lives in. Each row, objective term and variable carries an EQ. / OBJ. / VAR. header with its formula, so grep -rn "EQ. ─── maxFlow" flextool/engine finds an equation.

    What lives where. model/core.py holds the core model that every run uses: node balance, storage state, flows and capacity, conversion, investment maxima, CO2 caps, user constraints, profiles and the core objective. An optional feature that brings its own variables or rows lives in its own module, exposing the parts of the interface it needs — add_variables(b), add_constraints(b), objective_terms(b): _online (unit commitment), _unit_floors (minimum load and reserve headroom), _reserve, _group_slack (capacity margin, inertia, non-synchronous share), _cumulative_invest (investment floors and group / cumulative / flow-group limits), _commodity_ladder, _dc_power_flow, _exact (direction switch of exact connections), _delay (delayed inputs, used by conversion_indirect) and _non_anticipativity (stochastic ties). Small helpers shared by these live in _common. _capacity builds the capacity in service once per solve (b.cap, below); every family that reads an investment or a retirement takes its frames and sums from there.

    Emit order is part of the LP. Row and column order decide which alternate optimum the solver returns, so moving a step in the table of contents (or a variable in _declare_variables) changes results on degenerate models. Check the effect with tools/lp_snapshot.py (CONTRIBUTING.md "LP snapshot gate").

    Dense axes. _prepare also declares FlexTool's dense trailing axes, m.declare_dense_axes(("d", "t")) — (period, timestep). polar-high then builds the canonical matrix by the block-COO slice path: the pre-sorted dense axis is sliced as contiguous numpy blocks and the coefficient factors are multiplied with ufuncs, instead of wide relational joins; Sum-wrapped chains (the Sum-heavy nodeBalance / reserves / co2 families) evaluate in one pass via a captured SumBlockMeta recipe. The output is bit-identical to the plain polars path; the gain is the build's peak memory and wall time.

    Equation headers

    Every row family, variable and objective term carries a header comment directly above its add_cstr / add_var call (or the term it returns), and the generated formulation page is built from these headers (flextool/docs_build/formulation.py, the one reader of this grammar; tests/docs/test_header_grammar.py checks it):

            # ─── EQ. ─── maxOnline_{linear,integer} ──────────────────────────────
        # ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        # v_online[p,d,t] − Σ v_invest_p[p,d′] + Σ v_divest_p[p,d′]  ≤ existing_count[p,d]
        #   ∀ (p,d,t) ∈ p_idx
        # Units online cannot exceed the units built.
    • First line: # ─── KIND. ─── names ───, KIND one of EQ, VAR, OBJ. When the names do not fit, continuation lines follow: # and 13 spaces, then more names, until a line that ends in ─.
    • Names are separated by /, , or ;. A (…) part is a qualifier and is dropped (v_flow (coarse processes) is v_flow); a label: before a name is dropped (<family>_coarse: maxFlow_coarse). {a,b} expands to each alternative, [_x] is optional (co2_max_total[_path] is co2_max_total and co2_max_total_path). {sfx} is the one declared placeholder (_linear, _integer); <family> appears only in a label. Each expanded name is an identifier: the row or column name the LP carries (OBJ: the term's name).
    • Free-text header: a header whose names are words (# ─── OBJ. ─── reserve shortfall ───) carries the names on the next line, # names: a, b.
    • Body: the comment lines that follow, up to the first line that is not a comment or the next header; a line of ─ is a rule and is skipped. The body starts with the formula (the row, ∀ its domain, then the symbols it uses, indented) and continues with the prose from the first unindented line that starts with a capitalised word (Forces …, Row rule …). An indented line in the prose is a formula fragment.
    • One header per family. A generic row helper carries no header; its callers do (ROW_HELPERS in the test: _coarse_flows.add_cstr / add_twin / add_row_rule_cstr, _lead_time.period_limit, core._ramp_family, _cumulative_invest._group_period_limit_online, _online._add_max_input_flow_online).
    • Ratchets (tests/docs/test_header_grammar.py): every add_cstr / add_var in engine/model/ has a header earlier in its function or sits in a row helper whose callers have one; every autoscale CONSTRAINT_FAMILIES / VARIABLE_FAMILIES key is a header name, its _coarse twin, or a key lookup_cstr reaches by suffix (PREFIX_REGISTERED); every _obj_* step and objective_terms has an OBJ. header.

    Capacity symbols used in the headers

    For a unit, connection or storage node e in period d:

    • capacity[e,d] = existing[e,d] × virtual_unitsize[e] when virtual_unitsize is set, else existing[e,d] (MW, or MWh for storage). With virtual_unitsize, existing is the number of units.
    • unitsize[e] = virtual_unitsize[e], else existing (the largest period value), else 1000.
    • existing_count[e,d] = capacity[e,d] / unitsize[e]: equal to existing when virtual_unitsize is set; otherwise 1 (less in a period whose existing is below the largest, 0 without existing capacity).
    • invest_*, retire_*, cumulative_*, invest_forced and the capacity a construction lead time carries are MW (MWh) in every case. v_invest and v_divest are continuous, in units of unitsize.
    • The capacity in service (model/_capacity.py, built once per solve from the cohort table preprocess/params/_cohorts.py), in units of unitsize (state_unitsize for a storage node):
    • invested[e,d] = Σ_{d′ : (e,d′,d) ∈ alive} v_invest[e,d′]: the investments in service in d — the investment cohort (e,d′) is alive in d when y_on ≤ y[d] < y_eol, along the scenario lineage of a fan. y_on is the start of operation (existing capacity: y_on = y[d0] − age, d0 the first period of the run, age from age_at_model_start or model.start_year − build_year) and y_eol = round_end_of_life(y_on + lifetime_technical, end_of_life_method), the lifetime_technical of the period the capacity comes online (d0 for existing capacity) (_run_calendar.round_end_of_life: the nearer period start for closest_seam, the default, a tie to the later one; the one at or after it for next_seam, at or before it for previous_seam; within YEAR_TOL; +inf for reinvest_automatic);
    • prebuilt[e,d]: the same over the cohorts in service from the start of d (y[d] > y_on), not starting to operate in d;
    • retired[e,d] = Σ_{d′ : (e,d′,d) ∈ retired} v_divest[e,d′] + v_divested_built[e,d]: a retirement of existing capacity decided in d′ counts in d when y[d′] ≤ y[d] and the existing cohort of e is still in service in d (none for reinvest_automatic, which has no end of life); v_divested_built[e,d] is the model-built capacity retired so far that still counts in d (model/_retire_built.py, divestedBuilt: the retirements v_divest_built[e,c,d′] of each model-built cohort c of a reinvest_choice / no_reinvestment entity, (d′,d) ∈ dd_forward, while c is in service in d; one column per (entity, period) whatever the number of cohorts; absent without such a cohort, so existing-only rows keep their terms). A cohort retires at most what it has (maxDivest_cohort: the investment of this solve, or the carried size less the carried retirements); a chain carries each retirement with its cohort (handoff realized_retire), so the existing capacity's limit (maxRetired_existing) counts only the existing cohort's;
    • retired[e,d] also subtracts v_extended[e,d], the capacity extended beyond its end of life that is in service in d (model/_extend.py, extendedCapacity: each extension v_extend[e,c,d′] of a cohort c reaching its end of life at the start of an investment period d′, (d′,d) ∈ dd_forward, while y[d] < y_eol(x) of the extension cohort x = extend:<d′>:<c>). It is a capacity term, never an investment: the investment limits and constraint_invested_capacity_coeff do not count it. A cohort extends at most what it has in service at its end of life (maxExtend_cohort), once;
    • cap[e,d] = existing[e,d] + invested[e,d] − retired[e,d].
    • existing_mw[e,d] (p_entity_all_existing, MW or MWh; the existing of cap is existing_mw / unitsize) — the capacity a solve starts from, from the cohorts (_cohort_handoff.existing_capacity, emitted per solve by _chain_params.emit_existing_capacity): existing_mw[e,d] = pre[e,d] + carried[e,d] − retired_carried[e,d], with pre[e,d] = capacity[e,d] while the existing cohort (counted from the start of the run) is in service in d, carried[e,d] the cohorts earlier solves of the run realised (handoff realized_cohort) in service in d, and retired_carried[e,d] their realised retirements still counting in d (handoff realized_retire). The same formula in every solve: the first, a chained, a roll, a nested sub-solve; the user's existing is read for each period of each solve.
    • The price of an investment cohort per year in service (a_cap, CUR/MW a year, MWh for storage; column a_cap of the cohort table, carried by realized_cohort; _npv._per_method_annuity_lf, _annuity.level_factor), all values read at the period c the capacity comes online: A = invest_cost[e,c]·1000·r/(1 − (1+r)^−n)·(1+r)^(off_inv − 1) (c·1000/n for r = 0), r = discount_rate[e,c], n = payback_time[e,c] (else lifetime_technical[e,c]), off_inv = model.discount_offset_investment; a_cap = A·G(n)/G(L) with L = lifetime_technical[e,c], G(x) = Σ_{k<⌈x⌉} min(1, x−k)·(1+i)^−k, i = model.social_discount_rate (the investment payments are weighed by inflation_inv[d], the social discount factor of investment payments, at discount_offset_investment) (G(x) = x for i = 0); a_cap = A when n = L. The loan's present value spread evenly over the real lifetime: one price for every build period, rounding and period length. The timing factor (1+r)^(off_inv − 1) (_annuity.annuity_factor) makes the loan worth invest_cost at r for every discount_offset_investment. The salvage credit a_salv = S·(1+r)^−L·af(r, L) (af the annual factor annuity_factor, _annuity.salvage_credit) with S = 1000·net_salvage_value[e,d_eol] (_npv._level_prices_lf) lowers the price: the objective charges a_cap − a_salv over the cohort's periods in service (invest_cost_p OBJ header). A reinvest_automatic cohort is priced by its generation rows: each plant of the chain at the values of the period it comes online (_npv.generation_prices, chain_lumps; one price for the whole chain with constant values, the annuity bit for bit); the replacement of existing capacity as one plant (existing_replacement_prices), outside the default objective (existing_replacement OBJ header), and its retirement settled by chain_retirement_costs. The cohort also carries the loan (pay = A, n_pay = n, r_loan = r) and nsv_eol = S. A life extension (kind extend, parent the cohort it extends) is priced as one plant of its own (_extend.extension_prices): capital E + S_c (the life_extension_cost and the parent's salvage value it gives up), loan over life_extension_years at its discount_rate, its own salvage at the extended end of life; its option costs (a_cap − a_salv)·S̄ and its fixed cost over its periods in service (extend_cost OBJ header), and its retirement is one of model-built capacity. The existing cohort carries r_loan = r_x, a_salv = a_x and nsv_eol = S_x of its salvage credit (_existing_salvage.existing_salvage, divest_cost_p / existing_salvage OBJ headers).

    Part-load curves (piecewise_linear)

    User docs: units.md "Part-load efficiency curve". A unit with conversion_method piecewise_linear has a curve piecewise_linear_efficiency (load share ℓ → efficiency η; one curve, or one per period; no time level). Its fuel use per unit of capacity is F(ℓ) = ℓ/η(ℓ), linear between the points; convexity (non-falling incremental heat rate) is checked before the solve, so the curve is the upper envelope of its segment lines.

    • Curve → lines (engine/input/_piecewise_curve.py, no FlexData dependency, shared by both classifiers and the load boundary so they cannot drift): segment k has intercept β_k = ℓ_{k−1}ℓ_k(1/η_{k−1} − 1/η_k)/(ℓ_k − ℓ_{k−1}) (input per unit of capacity at zero output) and slope α_k = 1/η_k − β_k/ℓ_k; collinear points merge. Online form (the unit has online variables): the segments, plus an origin line (1/η₀, 0) when the efficiency falls from the first point. Capacity form (no online variables): the origin line through the best point (1/max η, 0) plus the segments with β < 0, exact for a capacity used as a fleet (min_U U·f(x/U) with U ≤ built). Both forms give the same full-load fuel F(1).
    • Classes (whole authored curve): one flat line → constant efficiency (p_slope = 1/η); one rising line → a process_min_load_eff member (p_slope = α, p_section = β; byte-identical to min_load_efficiency); two or more lines in some period → process_piecewise (fork forced, so the input has its own v_flow). A rising first segment gives automatic linear start-ups in both classifiers.
    • Load boundary (preprocess/params/_piecewise.apply_piecewise_curves, in load_flextool after the DB overrides, on every path: single, rolling, synthetic, nested, stochastic): reads the points through _param_shapes (MAP_LOAD / MAP_PERIOD_LOAD; branch periods inherit the anchor's curve; a solve period without a curve raises), writes p_piecewise_slope / p_piecewise_intercept (p, seg, d) and p_piecewise_points, overwrites the seeded p_slope / p_section in place and recomputes the input capacity Params derived from them. One-input, one-output multi-line units (process_piecewise_remap) keep their input-side items on the output (_projection.single_input_curve_arc_remap); reserves, operating cost, start-up input, commodity / CO2, flowGroup membership and inertia stay on the input.
    • Model (core._eq_conversion_piecewise, rows conversion_piecewise and the coarse twin): weighted input ≥ pw_slope·x + pw_intercept·unitsize·U for every line, U = units online, or the units built (existing count + invested − divested) without online variables; conversion_indirect skips these units. The rows are ≥, so the input can exceed the curve where that costs nothing or pays (the over-burn cases, units.md "Input above the curve"). Part-load measures of the reserve sizing use the lines (_online._fuel_share).
    • Detail reduction (_reduce_detail._full_load_piecewise): online* runs the unit as a constant_efficiency unit at its full-load efficiency (it leaves process_piecewise and the line fields; p_slope is the full-load fuel per weighted output, 1/η(1) at W = 1; the input limits stay; process_piecewise_remap stays, so a one-input unit keeps its reserve pairing at p_slope). Under integers* the curve is an online use when its efficiency rises from the first point (process_piecewise_needs_online); a unit without that use gets the capacity-form lines (_fold_piecewise, the same least fuel).
    • Checks. Before the solve: input_derivation/_validators.validate_piecewise_linear_inputs (E1–E10, E12; E11 in the reader; the warnings W1–W7, W7 = setups that invite input above the curve). After the solve: process_outputs/calc_piecewise measures per solve, in read_parameters, the weighted input and its part above the curve (process_piecewise_input_dt / process_piecewise_excess_dt, solve-keyed, persisted per roll and unioned like the other parameters) and write_outputs logs W5 per unit (steps with excess > 1e-5·max(1, q), the largest and the total). No result table.

    In a stochastic solve every forecast branch period is an rp period with the timeset of the period it branches from (_solve_writers._with_branch_period_timesets); rp_period_previous is then a tree (a branch's first period ← the trunk period, later periods ← the same branch). A plain bind_within_solve / bind_within_period / bind_intraperiod_blocks node there is a warning (_run_model._warn_plain_cyclic_in_rp_solve: the plain cycle moves the level between the representative periods unweighted while their flows are weighted, which is reasonable with equal weights or storage whose inflow timing does not matter; the weights are the user's to use).

    The three blended-weights methods that activate the block — bind_within_solve_blended_weights, bind_within_period_blended_weights, and bind_forward_only_blended_weights — are projected from the user-side storage_binding_method value list by storage_bind_within_solve_blended_weights / storage_bind_within_period_blended_weights / storage_bind_forward_only_blended_weights in _projection.py, which populate the corresponding FlexData fields. nodeState_rp is the union (the inter-period constraints all share it); the cyclic constraint further filters back to the within-solve / within-period subset because the forward-only variant deliberately leaves the chain open.

    The on-disk rp_weights carrier is authored as a flat triple (base_start, rep_start, weight) and decoded by _timeline._decode_rp_weights, which accepts both the nested Map shape and the flattened list-of-Maps shape Spine produces depending on import path. Both decode to the same nested {base: {rep: weight}} dict that _solve_writers._compute_rp_frames converts into the per-solve rp_weights frame and from which it derives the rp_base_chain, rp_base_first, rp_base_last, rp_block_* and rp_base_period_set companion sets, one set of rows per period of the solve (every frame has a period column), plus rp_period_previous (the rp periods in solve order). solve/_run_model.py emits them for every period whose timeset has representative_period_weights; periods without keep their timeset_weights rows in the same timestep_weight frame. The forecast branch periods of a stochastic solve are rp periods with the timeset of the period they branch from (tests/engine/test_invest_branching_rp.py). A rolling solve on representative periods (_reject_rolling_rp) and a solve that mixes rp and other periods while it has blended-weights nodes (_check_rp_solve) stop with an error.

    Vectorized per-roll emit

    The heavy per-step parameter families — pdtProcess, pdtNode, the pdtProcess_{source,sink} per-side variants, pdtCommodity, pdtGroup, and friends — each produce a dense (entity × dt) (or (entity × period)) frame. They are derived with vectorized polars, once per roll, over that roll's own window: there is no cache and no full-domain frame.

    The recipe is the same for every family. First build the dense grid for the roll with build_entity_dt_grid (or build_entity_period_grid for the period-only families) from _vectorize.py. The grid is the cross-product of the entity list and the roll's (period, time) list, and it carries integer order keys (__eo for the entity position, __to / __po for the time or period position) so that a final .sort on those keys reproduces the intended entity-major, time-within-entity row order. Next, lift each parameter source into a small polars lookup frame with lift_dict_to_lookup and left-join it onto the grid. Where a family also has stochastic or parent-period terms, build_fold_frame computes those as a group-by-sum and joins the result in as one more candidate column. Then coalesce_value picks the first non-null candidate across the cascade branches in priority order, and collect_value_frame collects the graph to a single Float64 value column, sorts by the order keys, and renders each value to a string. derive_pdtProcess_vectorized in _pdt_params.py is a representative example, and emit_pdtProcess simply publishes its output to the Provider.

    Rules that keep the rendered output exact:

    • Render values with the repr() loop, never .cast(Utf8). _render_value_column walks the value column and emits repr(v) per cell. A polars .cast(Utf8) diverges from repr on scientific-notation exponent padding and on NaN/nan, which would silently corrupt the emitted text.
    • Lift lookups from the de-duplicated parameter dict, not raw CSV. The parameter dicts the family already builds are last-wins-deduped. Lifting a lookup frame straight from the raw CSV would re-introduce duplicate join keys and explode the left-join (one grid row matching many lookup rows). Always lift from the dict.
    • Preserve order. Keep the domain / period order of the source lists (don't .unique() them) and sort on the __eo / __to keys.
    • Parity gate. Every heavy derive has a tests/engine/test_vectorize_<family>_parity.py against a reference implementation: Tier A .equals for pure lookup / coalesce families, Tier B rtol≈1e-12 (both sides parsed back to float) for many-term sums.

    The time-series sources of the folds (pt_*, pbt_*, pdt_process, pdt_node, pdt_energy_margin_adder, and pd_energy_margin_adder, passed through verbatim) are read from solve_data/, never from input/: create_averaged_timeseries writes them there, combined to the solve's steps when the solve has new_stepduration (architecture.md Solve-step aggregation) and verbatim otherwise. A derive and its _vectorized twin switch a source in the same commit, and their parity fixtures seed the solve_data/ key. The input/pt_node_inflow reads of the peak scaling stay on input/: peak_inflow is per original step; so does the input/pbt_node_inflow read of the inflow-scaling checks, which takes only the node names (the same in both folders).

    Topological ordering

    Emitters have dependencies — _chain_params.emit_existing_capacity reads realized_cohort from prior solves' handoffs, _existing reads pd_invest_set from _period_params; _fixed_costs reads ed_lifetime_fixed_cost which depends on p_entity_all_existing; _block's overlap sets feed the arc-side block aggregation in _arc_unions.

    The runner does not compute the order. It is encoded in the call order of _apply_db_overrides (in flex_data.py) and the load sequence in load_flextool. Adding a new derived field typically means appending one call to the right phase.

    FlexDataProvider and override translator

    FlexDataProvider (preprocess/_flex_data_provider.py, not re-exported) is the in-memory dict-of-frames that every cascade module reads from and writes to; all cross-phase data moves through it, there is no disk-side hand-off inside the cascade. Keys are declared in _provider_keys.py (no .csv suffix); the convention is <parent>/<basename> (e.g. solve_data/foo, input/bar, handoff/realized_cohort). Bare-basename consumer lookups are resolved against the parent-qualified key, so a single put registers the frame under one canonical name.

    Prefix Purpose
    INPUT_* Backend-derived rows the cascade re-reads each iteration (CSV-shaped, populated from the input DB).
    SOLVE_DATA_* Per-solve sets and parameters that the derivation layer produces (the bulk of the preprocess/emit/ output).
    HANDOFF_* / OVERRIDE_* Cross-solve handoff carriers (Solve-to-solve handoff) and their override siblings; see below.

    Override translator

    _provider_translators.py carries two small layers that let the orchestrator inject external state into the live Provider without re-running the emit pipeline (user guide: Injecting parameters between solves):

    • translate_handoff_to_provider(handoff, provider) writes one HANDOFF_* key per field of a SolveHandoff. Empty fields land as header-only frames (_HANDOFF_EMPTY_SCHEMAS) so consumers can use a single provider.get(K.HANDOFF_X).height > 0 check.
    • translate_overrides_to_provider(overrides, provider) takes a caller-supplied {K.HANDOFF_X: pl.DataFrame, ...} dict and writes each frame under the corresponding OVERRIDE_X key. Unknown keys are rejected with ValueError so the override surface stays explicit.

    Consumers go through read_handoff_frame(provider, K.HANDOFF_X), which checks the OVERRIDE_X slot first and falls back to the natural HANDOFF_X carrier. A non-empty override shadows the cascade-produced handoff for that key; an empty override frame falls through cleanly, so the override layer is opt-in per key.

    Enum-dtype axis convention

    Axis-Enum mode is EXPERIMENTAL and off in production: production runs keep every axis column pl.Utf8. FLEXTOOL_AXIS_ENUMS=1 (exactly "1") turns it on for a whole run_chain_from_db run and logs a warning once; tests switch it on per load with load_scenario(axis_enums=True). In this mode identity columns in FlexData (entity, source, sink, period, time, group, commodity, …) carry the pl.Enum dtype rather than pl.Utf8. The vocabulary is built by spinedb_backend._axis_enums.build_scenario_axis_enums (lexically sorted categories, so an Enum run builds the byte-identical LP of the Utf8 run; rules in flextool/schemas/AXIS_CONTRACT.md), attached at the SpineDBBackend and SpineDbReader emit boundaries, inherited by every sub-solve provider, and propagated through the cascade by _axis_enums.cast_flexdata_axes / cast_value_axes. The guard is the LP parity of the two modes: tests/engine/test_axis_enum_lp_parity_sites.py (normal suite, one fixture per site family), the opt-in pytest -m axis_enums tests/engine/axis_enums -n 8 (nightly, non-gating CI job enum-mode), and by hand tools/lp_snapshot.py snapshot A, snapshot B --axis-enums, compare A B.

    Known gaps (not exercised, or not Enum, in this mode):

    • Writing results (process_outputs) is not covered by the opt-in run. It runs after the run's vocabulary is reset, so its axis columns come out as Categorical instead of String; a manual base check found the output values equal to the Utf8 run's.
    • Providers built without a vocabulary stay Utf8 even with the switch on: region decomposition (decomposition/region_decomposition.py) and the CLI region export (decomposition/region_filter.py), the staged input loader (engine/input/_db_loader.py), the CSV reload (engine/_csv_reload/load.py), and the sub-solve provider that _run_model.py builds when the run state carries none.
    • Engine Benders inherits the vocabulary through FlexData (_region_filter.py widens it per region), but no lp_snapshot fixture runs it; only the Benders / master-hosted region tests that load with axis_enums=True cover it.
    • Intended narrowing casts null labels outside the scenario's vocabulary: d casts of periods outside the solve (_retire_settlement.py, _derived.py) and the constraint cast in _spinedb_reader.py. No LP effect in the fixtures; a site that needs such a label must not read it through the cast.

    The column-axis helpers in engine/input/_axis_enums.py make the Enum preservation mechanical at the call sites where polars would otherwise drop back to Utf8:

    • rename_to_axis(frame, mapping) — rename columns AND cast to the matching axis Enum in one call. Any rename that introduces a canonical axis column ("n", "p", "e", "source", "sink", "period", …) takes ownership of the dim-column dtype at the rename target.
    • alias_to_axis(source, target_axis) — the sibling for select()/with_columns() projections that rename via alias instead of .rename(...). Accepts either a string column name or an arbitrary pl.Expr.
    • lit_axis(value, axis) — pl.lit(value) that emits the canonical axis Enum dtype, so literal tokens injected into axis-aware columns don't SchemaError against neighbouring Enum frames.
    • join_aligned(left, right, ...) / filter_isin(frame, col, values) — join and membership across axis columns of different vocabularies (see "Entity-union axis" below).
    • schema_dtype(enums, axis) — for empty-frame fallbacks: the Enum dtype when a live vocabulary is set, pl.Utf8 otherwise. Never hard-code schema={"e": pl.Utf8, ...}.

    Entity-union axis

    Mixed-vocab dimension columns ("source", "sink", and anywhere a join needs to land both sides of a heterogeneous arc) resolve through the axis-synonym table to the canonical axis "e", a union enum that covers every entity vocabulary. When joining frames whose dim columns hold different vocabulary subsets, use the union axis named "entity" and cast against the union enum. Never mix Enum and Utf8 columns in the same join — the polars caster nulls out the mismatched side silently. _axis_enums.align_join_dtypes normalises both sides before the join where the call site cannot use one of the rename helpers.

    A column named for an axis ("p", "n", "d", …) carries that axis's Enum: narrow an entity-union frame onto the axis by renaming and casting it to the axis dtype (rename_to_axis, or _cumulative_invest._on_var_axis against a variable's own column). Period labels carry the d Enum wherever they are joined to a period-keyed frame, also in a column named b (the forecast star of _non_anticipativity). Only an entity-union role column (source / sink / attr_node) is matched against a narrower axis column, through join_aligned(left, right, on=… | left_on=…, right_on=…) or, for membership, filter_isin(frame, col, values). Both act only on pl.Enum keys: the right key (the values) is cast to the left key's Enum, so inner / left / semi / anti keep every left row and dtype; an Enum against a Utf8 key raises TypeError (type the Utf8 side where it is built — a Utf8 cast "bridge" is not a fix); how="full" / "cross" raise. Utf8 keys (production) are the plain join / is_in. The rule: never .join, .is_in or pl.concat axis columns that may hold different vocabularies without join_aligned / filter_isin or first casting both to one target dtype.

    Per-solve sets and PDT lookup

    A FlexTool scenario may have several solves with overlapping but distinct time / entity scopes. The per-solve scoping machinery lives in two places:

    • preprocess/params/_per_solve_sets.py — produces per-active-solve aggregates directly from InputSource: period_in_use, dt_complete, period__timeline, p_timeline_duration_in_years, complete_period_share_of_year. These are the sets that decide which timesteps and periods the next LP build will see. They are computed once per load_flextool / per orchestration step and reused throughout.
    • preprocess/emit/_pdt_lookup.py — the period × time-instant parameter family (pdtProcess, pdtNode, the per-side variants, …). For each parameter there is a priority cascade: a period+time default falls back to a period default, then a time default, then a class-level default, with stochastic and parent-period folds layered on top. The derive is vectorized and runs per roll over the roll's own window (Vectorized per-roll emit).

    Together these two modules resolve "logical model time" (period identifiers like y2025, branch suffixes like y2025_low) against the "physical timeline" — the ordered-by-step timeline that HiGHS sees through polar_high.

    Solve modes

    The same build_flextool body backs three solver wrappers exported by polar_high. They differ only in what they do between solves.

    Monolithic — polar_high.Problem

    The default. One LP, one HiGHS run. Used for every single-shot solve and for the cold rebuild path inside a cascade. The include_existing_fixed_cost flag on build_flextool toggles the inclusion of the existing-capacity fixed-cost constant in the objective; default False to match the parquet writer (see the docstring on build_flextool).

    Warm-start cascade — polar_high.WarmProblem

    With warm=True (the default of the CLI, the GUI and run_chain_from_db) each sub-solve is solved in-process through a WarmProblem. The built LP is reused for the next sub-solve only when that sub-solve would build exactly the same LP with the same labels; the warm path pushes no updates. solve/_warm.py carries the decision:

    • _fingerprint(data) — a cheap pre-filter: the heights of the _STRUCTURAL_FIELDS sets plus the time-axis structure (_time_axis_signature). A mismatch means "build a new LP".
    • _reuse_blocker(prior, next) — the decision. Every FlexData field is compared by content (_values_equal: frames by schema and rows, Params by dims and frame, containers element by element), dt first; it returns the first differing field or None.
    • _apply_warm_updates(warm, prior, next) — raises _IncompatibleUpdate naming that field, or returns 0.

    The orchestrator (_drive_cascade) additionally requires the same solver configuration: HiGHS options, autoscale mode, user_bound_scale and the objective scale (solver_key). A reused LP keeps the options and the Layer-2 plan of its build, which is exact because the LP is identical.

    Why labels: a reused WarmProblem keeps the Var frames and the HiGHS column / row names of the sub-solve it was built for. The output writer (write_all_variables) and build_handoff_from_solution select the realized (d, t) of the current sub-solve by those labels, so an LP built for another window reports other hours (or zeros) and loses the storage / online / invest carry-over. Rolls shift dt on every roll and are therefore always rebuilt; the per-shape autoscale cache still skips the range walks. Reuse happens between consecutive identical non-last solves (only the last solve of a model that realizes a period gets realized_dispatch / dt_non_anticipativity).

    The in-process basis cache (FLEXTOOL_WARM_START, NamedBasis, policy "exact") is keyed on the exact column / row name set, so a roll is warm-started only from the same roll of an earlier run, never from the previous roll. Roll-to-roll LP reuse (adopting the new labels on the live LP) and roll-to-roll basis transfer are not implemented. --save-memory disables warm start (architecture.md Solver layout).

    The OrchestrationStep.warm_used boolean reports whether each sub-solve re-solved the previous LP or was built cold.

    Spatial Benders — master + multi-cut loop

    solve/_benders.solve_benders slices FlexData by region using _region_filter.split, which returns a list of RegionSplit (one per region) plus the HalfFlow / Coupling metadata that describes cross-region arcs. A coordinating master is built by build_flextool over a network-only reduced FlexData (_region_filter.master_network_data) and wrapped in a polar_high.WarmProblem; it holds the trade flows f, the trade-connection invest C, and one recourse var η_r per region (WarmProblem.add_recourse_col). Each iteration pins every region's forward trade columns to the master's chosen f̄, solves the region as an operational sub-problem, reads the pinned columns' reduced costs, and appends one optimality cut per region (WarmProblem.add_cut_row). The loop converges on the optimality gap; the master objective is a valid lower bound.

    The FlexTool wrapper handles the FlexTool-side concerns: regional unitsize, regional profiles, regional handoff state, and the RP-weight consistency that follows from building the master with the same emit as the monolith. After the loop the solve's results come from the final solve: the orchestrator falls through to the monolithic path with the investment decisions pinned to the Benders incumbent (_benders_final.pin_invest_decisions); with --benders-final-solve off a chained Benders solve writes only its investment slice (output/_benders_slice.py). See decomposition.md (§2.9 for the outputs) for the user-facing story and the parameters that gate this path. flextool/decomposition/ holds the CLI-side region filter (architecture.md decomposition).

Under csv_dump=True the four solve_data/ mirrors p_entity_divested, fix_storage_quantity, fix_storage_usage, p_roll_continue_state (process_outputs/handoff_writers.py) and the provider keys are written for inspection; nothing reads them.

    After a solve the handoff/* keys keep what that solve consumed (the translated and routed carriers), so load_flextool(work_folder, provider=<post-solve provider>, db_reader=<the run's reader>) (tests._support.load.load_scenario), or the unsupported reload of the provider's csv dump (_csv_reload.load_from_dump), rebuilds the LP that was solved. The solve's own (produced) SolveHandoff is written beside them under produced_handoff/* (translate_produced_handoff_to_provider; e.g. the final produced_handoff/cumulative_commodity.csv of a rolling ladder); nothing in the cascade reads those keys.

    Unit-commitment continuity across a solve boundary is hard-enforced and timeline-aware: a min_uptime / min_downtime obligation opened near a solve's last realized step carries exactly where dt_ramp_carry does — into the first steps of the next solve that continue that step on the timeline (jump == 1): the same period, a period that continues the previous one, and the forecast branches that start with such a step. It does not carry into a period that does not continue the previous one (investment years that reuse one timeset, e.g. 2020 → 2025), across a gap or into a representative-block start. The consumer counts elapsed time from the dt_ramp_carry roots along dt_ramp_link and adds each history step's hours_to_end (params/_mintime.py); it raises when the history does not end at the carried step (history and step handed over from different solves). Time-keyed carries (ramp, online, min up / down) reach forecast branches through the dt_*_carry roots (_ramp_links._with_fan_members); the period-keyed branch_periods_inherit_carry (params/_branch.py) is deliberately not applied to them — hours_to_end plus the roots is its timeline counterpart, and a copy per branch would count twice. A solve whose first step continues a step of another time resolution (a 6 h storage roll after an hourly dispatch) has no dt_ramp_carry there: it takes neither the ramp nor the min up / down carry, and the cut-short look-back warning names the step. A node with the default penalty_method (regular) absorbs any forced-on generation through its downward penalty (vq_state_down, a feasible spill); a hard-balance node (penalty_method = off) can instead become infeasible when a myopically-committed unit's forced generation cannot be absorbed — the model correctly refusing an un-absorbable commitment, resolved by allowing slack.

    Realization-solve carriers. A realization solve (the deterministic solve after a stochastic one) takes the PIN_FIELDS carriers of its parent: the investments and fix_online (the NA-tied units online of the processes in process_online_fix_in_uc, startup_method linear_fix_in_uc / integer_fix_in_uc: online_fix_eq_linear / online_fix_eq_integer; the units online of linear and integer processes are re-decided). It does not take reserve: it meets the reserve requirements with its own data. A per-unit reserve pin was tried and removed; a later reserve-market stage that realizes reserve separately must handle why it failed: reserve provision has no cost, so the stochastic solve's per-unit allocation is degenerate (solver noise); an == pin is infeasible against the profile, storage-reserve and ramp rows when the realized data is lower; a requirement that varies with the data (stochastic reservation, dynamic, n-1) is set day-ahead in a real market, so the stage has to choose between the procured and the recomputed amount; and the per-unit day-ahead tie (non_anticipativity_reserve, kept) assumes non-transferable awards. Such a carrier (fix_online is the pattern) needs:

    • one row in SolveHandoff._FIELDS, PIN_FIELDS, _FIELD_TO_KEY and _HANDOFF_EMPTY_SCHEMAS, and a handoff/... key constant in preprocess/_provider_keys.py (HANDOFF_FIX_ONLINE);
    • a producer: online_tie_cells / reference_forecasts are the pattern;
    • a FlexData field (p_fix_online) and its loader from the handoff key;
    • one entry in flex_data.coarse_pin_varies;
    • its constraint, with an EQ. header and a CONSTRAINT_FAMILIES entry in autoscale/_layer2_types.py;
    • the field in the _reduce_detail field list of the detail level that leaves its variable out (p_fix_online sits with the online fields; a reserve carrier goes in RESERVE_FIELDS).

    How the fix_storage carriers enter the child solve

    The three fix_storage_* carriers translate into distinct LP wirings on the child solve:

    • fix_storage_quantity pins v_state[n, d_upper, t_upper] * p_state_unitsize[n] to the parent's realized state — a hard equality at the boundary timesteps. The node_storage_fix constraint family is gated on p_fix_storage_quantity being populated; the supporting sets n_fix_storage_quantity and ndt_fix_storage_quantity are derived from the Param frame at load time.

    • fix_storage_price arrives on the child as a dual carrier folded into p_storage_state_reference_price. The objective term added by use_reference_price (model._obj_storage_reference_price) values v_state at the last step of each period_last period (and of a chained block tail, model._horizon_end_steps) by that reference price, so the parent's shadow price on storage at the boundary becomes the child's end-of-period valuation:

      − Σ_{n ∈ nodeState, (d, t) ∈ period__time_last : d ∈ period_last} p_storage_state_reference_price[n, d] · v_state[n, d, t] · unitsize[n] · rp_cost_weight[d, t] · inflation_op[d] / period_share[d] · pdt_branch_weight[d, t]

    with inflation_op[d] (p_inflation_op, the social discount factor of operating costs) = Σ over the years of d of the years' length · (1 + social_discount_rate)^−(years from the run start + discount_offset_operations).

    The same parameter is also populated for any node whose storage_binding_method is use_reference_price, so the routing is unified and the dual carrier merely supplies values for the existing term. - fix_storage_usage adds the node_storage_usage_fix_le constraint (model._eq_storage_usage_fix, gated on n_fix_storage_usage / ndt_fix_storage_usage / p_fix_storage_usage all being populated). The constraint pins cumulative realized usage at nodeState_last_dt to be at most the parent's allowance, indexed over the n_fix_storage_usage subset of nodes.

    On the producer side build_handoff_from_solution sources each metric independently — quantity from v_state at fix-storage timesteps, price from the fix_storage_* row duals, usage from the realised flow integrals — so a fixture exercising one metric doesn't force the others to be populated. The OVERRIDE_* siblings of HANDOFF_FIX_STORAGE_QUANTITY / _PRICE / _USAGE let the orchestrator inject parent-imposed values without re-solving the parent.

    Storage carry routing (where a sub-solve's storage starts)

    roll_end_state is the state (MWh) at the last realised step of a sub-solve. Which sub-solve's end state a later sub-solve continues from is decided in one place, solve/_carry_routing.py (CarryRouter, run-scoped, created in _run_model.native_run_model):

    • every realised end is appended to a registry with the sub-solve's nesting level (0 at the top, +1 per contains_solve; an auto-spawned realised-dispatch sibling takes its parent's level) and the position of its realised end step in time;
    • a sub-solve continues, per node, from the latest entry recorded before it started at its own level or deeper whose end lies before its first step. That gives the next roll, the next solve of a sequence, the upward feedback (a storage roll continues from the dispatch below it), and the first dispatch roll under a later storage roll. A sibling's cut-off is its parent's start, so it never takes its parent's end-of-window state;
    • a level's first appearance has no entry: the node is "first" and starts from fix_start (or free).

    At iteration start _run_model writes the routed frame into both HANDOFF_ROLL_END_STATE and HANDOFF_UPWARD_ROLL_END_STATE (after the sequential and parent translates, before the external override translate, so an override still wins). In the model (model/_self_discharge.py) the carried nodes are the nodes of p_roll_continue_state: they get the carry terms (except cyclic and representative-period nodes) and no fix_start pin; the others get fix_start (or the open first step). p_nested_solve_first is not read by storage (it gates the divest history).

    The carriers tied to the realised end step (STEP_FIELDS: roll_end_online, roll_end_flow, roll_end_step, online_history) are routed by the same rule, taken whole from the latest entry with a realised step: the next dispatch roll continues the commitment, the ramp and the min up / down history from the dispatch realised last, also across a storage or investment solve in between. The producer extends the min up / down history it was handed (the routed handoff/online_history its LP read) when its realized chain reaches the step that continued from it (its dt_ramp_carry), adding that step's hours_to_end. A nested parent's handoff is translated only for PIN_FIELDS (realised investment / existing / divest, fixed online, the nested storage fix). The running totals (cumulative_co2, cumulative_commodity, cum_sim_hours) are not routed: every sub-solve passes them on with its own realised part added, so the sequential predecessor holds the total at any level.

    Where the storage chain ends

    period_last (solve_data/period_last, _solve_writers._compute_first_and_last_periods) is the solve's last period and its stochastic branch siblings (the period__branch rows of its anchor that end on the same timestep). An earlier period of a multi-period solve is not last even when it ends on the same timestep name (periods sharing one timeset). The horizon-end conditions of storage_solve_horizon_method (model._horizon_end_steps) apply at nodeState_last_dt ∩ period_last and, in a rolling solve, at the end of a chained bind_intraperiod_blocks tail that a looping period follows (_self_discharge.block_tail_ends: a period the roll holds in part, not last, from whose last step no block state link continues).

    fix_end / fix_start_end (storage_fix_end) pin the state at nodeState_last_dt ∩ period_last to storage_state_end of the built capacity (storage_state_end_binding, model._eq_storage_end_binding), in every sub-solve whose horizon holds the last step of the entire model. _run_model computes that flag per sub-solve (_carry_routing.reaches_model_end: the sub-solve's end — the base-timeline position after the last step of its periods, so an aggregated new_stepduration step covering the last hour counts and a stochastic solve ends with its terminal branches — equals the latest end over all sub-solves of the run; a sub-solve that cannot be placed in time does not reach it) and emits it as solve_data/storage_solve_at_model_end (FlexData.storage_solve_at_model_end, default True for a single solve or a FlexData built without the cascade). So an investment solve and the last dispatch roll that both run to the end are both pinned, also a roll whose look-ahead reaches it; each realised-dispatch sibling is judged on its own steps.

    Precedence at a step where several end conditions meet (model._nested_fixed_end): an upper-level nested fix wins over the end pin for fix_quantity (node_balance_fix_quantity_eq_lower, a second equality on the same state) and fix_usage (node_storage_usage_fix_le, which the pin could make infeasible), not for fix_price (an objective term). The horizon-end reference value (storage_state_solve_horizon_reference_value) yields to all three, fix_price included (ndt_fix_storage_price, the steps of handoff/fix_storage_price).

    A fix_start_end node ignores its storage_binding_method (reference/nodes.md, exception bind_forward_only): _run_model._fix_start_end_chains_forward rewrites the per-solve input/node__storage_binding_method frame right after _downgrade_rp_methods_for_non_rp_solve (so a blended node of a solve without representative periods goes blended → plain → forward-only), mapping bind_within_timeblock / _period / _solve to bind_forward_only and the *_blended_weights cycles to bind_forward_only_blended_weights. Every storage_bind_* set of the solve comes from that frame, and every sub-solve (rolling, nested, stochastic, the Benders region subproblems built from the solve's FlexData) passes that site. bind_intraperiod_blocks stays (it is an aggregation, not a cycle scope): model._self_discharge.block_forward_nodes (nodeStateBlock ∩ storage_fix_start ∩ storage_fix_end) chain their blocks forward in every period on block_forward_lag (the bind_forward_only lag on the block steps, the construction a rolling roll uses in a period it holds in part), with the start in the first block's balance and the end pin on the last block. So the start pin and the end pin never sit on one state: a cyclic node is pinned at its wrap target by fix_start only, and at its end by fix_end only. In a solve on representative periods the mapping is _FIX_START_END_FORWARD_RP: a bind_within_timeblock fix_start_end node becomes bind_forward_only_blended_weights (plain bind_forward_only there is a configuration error, _reject_plain_forward_in_rp_solve, checked on the database methods before this mapping).

    Then _run_model._timeblock_binds_within_period_in_roll rewrites bind_within_timeblock to bind_within_period in a rolling solve (_reject_rolling_with_gaps has made sure each period is one timeblock), so the per-period rolling rule (_scope_rows, scope_* rows) applies to it. Order per sub-solve: non-RP downgrade → _reject_rolling_rp / _reject_rolling_with_gaps / _warn_plain_cyclic_in_rp_solve / _reject_plain_forward_in_rp_solve → fix_start_end → rolling timeblock. _restore_methods captures the frame after these rewrites.

    Storage warnings and notices print one line per code and complete solve, naming how many nodes and the first (run_warnings.warn_grouped(code, names, solve=...): W-0534..W-0539, W-0580..W-0583; notice(..., solve=...)); the values per node go to the flextool.warnings logger at debug level.

    Flex-temporal decomposition

    A node of a group whose new_stepduration is longer than the solve's step is a coarse node. A coarse node is balanced once per coarse step by nodeBalanceCoarse_eq — nodeBalance_eq summed over the fine steps of the coarse step — and a coarse storage node keeps one state per coarse step (stateConstantCoarse_eq). No arc is filtered or weighted by block: every arc enters the balance of both its nodes. A unit or connection whose every balance node is coarse (processCoarse) has one flow, units online, start-up / shut-down, reserve and exact direction per coarse step (model/_coarse_flows: aliased levels, events at b_first, averaged <family>_coarse twins of its arc-local rows); rows coupling several entities follow the row rule (averaged per coarse step when every member is coarse, else per solve step with the coarse terms constant). Only a delay keeps a process at the solve's step. Readers expand coarse variables through engine/output/_coarse_flows (COARSE_VARS).

    • model/_coarse_flows — alias, domain, add_cstr (twins), add_row_rule_cstr / row_partition (row rule), ramp_rows.
    • params/_coarse.coarse_process_frames + flex_data.coarse_process_*, coarse_tie_straddles, coarse_pin_varies — which processes are coarse, which keep the solve's step and why (info line), and the notice reasons.
    • emit/_blocks.py / _block_layout.BlockLayout — per-solve node blocks (entity_block) and block step durations; the default block's period boundaries give nodeState_last_dt.
    • params/_coarse.coarse_node_frames — the one source of the coarse steps (nodeCoarse, node_coarse_step, node_coarse_interior), cut from the solve's own steps at gaps, timeset-block / period / roll starts, representative-block starts and a roll's realized end; called at the end of load_flextool for every (sub-)solve.
    • bind_intraperiod_blocks storage nodes (nodeStateBlock) are balanced per timeset block by nodeBalanceBlock_eq: nodeBalance_eq summed over the block, the state change through dtttdt_block_lag (state constant within the block, v_state of a block = its end state). The blocks of a period loop; in a rolling sub-solve the blocks are also cut at the realized end, and the periods the roll holds only in part (period_block_chain, from solve_data/roll_period_whole, written by _run_model) take the bind_forward_only lag instead, so the node is an open-start node there (_self_discharge.block_open_nodes).

    See decomposition.md § 3.

    Stochastic branches

    solve/_stochastic.StochasticSolver reads solve.stochastic_branches from the source DB and decorates each affected period with branch-suffixed clones (y2025 becomes y2025, y2025_low, y2025_high, …). Branches enter the active-time lists and get LP variables; only the realized branch contributes to realized_time_lists, fix_storage_time_lists and the handoff carriers.

    Behaviours to know:

    • Zero-weight branches: excluded from new_active_time_list but still appear in solve_branch__time_branch_lists under certain three-way conditions on branch != period and the realized flag.
    • Sticky branch suffixes across periods: when a roll's jump exceeds a period length, branches continue into subsequent periods reusing the same period + "_" + branch naming.
    • Single realized branch per period (otherwise loud failure).
    • Investments of the branch copies follow solve.invest_solve_branching_method (investment solves only; SolveConfig.invest_branching_method_for, refused elsewhere by check_invest_branching). The resolver feeds the internal switches the model reads: recourse_invest (written as solve_data/stochastic_invest_method.csv), the invest tie periods (invest_tie_periods_for → pd_non_anticipativity_invest_periods: the reveal period under investments_after_reveal_period, tied by add_invest_constraints) and the operational window (operational_window_for: empty for an investment solve, so nothing operational is tied; unset for a dispatch solve, which ties its committed window). Under operations_only the investments stay on the period labels. A per-branch investment is never realized; the handoff realizes the tied reveal-period copy under its label (flex_data._label_tied_cohorts), and the realization solve of an investment solve starts at the reveal (SolveConfig.realization_window).

    The non-anticipativity constraints (add_dispatch_constraints in _non_anticipativity.py) close the LP back together at the anchor period across sibling branches — there are four families covering v_state, v_online, v_reserve, and v_flow. The implementation pins the sibling-branch variables via Var renaming (d → b) so the engine's join routes the same Var through two key columns at once.

    Realization rolls

    A top-level rolling solve with stochastic_branches and an explicit dispatch_realization_method within_solve or separate_dispatch (_realization_tail.realization_roll_method; not with an author-declared realization solve, nested solves or delayed units) is solved as realization rolls R_k (roll_tail_plan → RollTail, keyed on the sub-solve). R_k dispatches its jump once with the realized: yes branch's data, read as one timeline (realized_analysis_times), under the period's own name (the label), and after the jump looks ahead with every forecast made at the end of the jump (roll_branch_rows): each forecast copy p_f holds the steps after the jump, so the path of forecast f is the jump plus p_f (the trunk). A roll whose horizon ends with its jump is deterministic (realized data only). within_solve: R_k is the only solve of the roll. separate_dispatch: solve (1) is the stochastic solve over the forecasts made at the roll start and ties only the units online of process_online_fix_in_uc (solve_data/na_online_fix_in_uc, na role uc_only); its realization solve is R_k with the pins over the jump. Single solves keep the realization solve over their realized periods.

    A split roll (a jump that ends inside a period) gives the base period both the jump steps and forecast copies after them, so per-period rows and step-pair relations must be path-aware. Four checks turn a missed case into an internal error, not a different LP: G1 (period-level rows, PERIOD_ROW_KINDS / REVIEWED_STEP_LEVEL in model/_period_rows.py), G2 (every (d, t) cell has a branch weight), G3 (no self-wrap) and G4 (the tail links to the jump end; both over STEP_RELATIONS in preprocess/_step_relations.py). They do nothing without a period label with steps. A new period-level constraint family and a new FlexData frame that links two steps must be registered there; the coverage tests refuse an unregistered one.

    Conventions worth knowing

    • Lazy at the rim, eager at the boundary. Internal frames are pl.LazyFrame; helpers .collect() once at the function boundary. This is the pattern documented at the top of _derived.py and _per_solve_sets.py.
    • None-skip is canonical. A field at None means "feature not active" and is the recommended way to opt out. Empty frames mean "active but no rows". Helpers should never silently coerce None into an empty frame.
    • Provider is authoritative; CSVs are debug-only (FlexDataProvider).
    • One funnel per source. All Provider reads inside emitter modules go through _provider_io._provider_key / _provider_lookup_positional. All Spine DB reads go through SpineDbReader.parameter(). Adding a new reader site is a smell; route through the existing funnel. Readers fetch only what the model instance uses.
    • Fail loud on schema surprises. Prefer FlexToolConfigError / ValueError / NotImplementedError over silent fallbacks: the engine walks back to the input the moment a shape doesn't match.
    • LP determinism. flextool/engine/__init__.py::_install_polar_high_determinism makes add_var / add_cstr sort their domains before assigning column / row ids; a script that builds an LP with polar_high directly must call it first. Axis vocabularies are lexically sorted; never let an order come from a Python set (it changes with PYTHONHASHSEED, and with it the simplex path and the alternate optimum). Check a suspected flake in separate processes.
