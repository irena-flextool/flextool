    Unit floors, input shares and reserve shortfall

    Engine notes for _unit_floors.py, model/core.py::_eq_min_input_share and _reserve.reserve_shortfall_scale. User-facing semantics are in docs/reference.md (min_load, capacity_min_coeff, input_share_min, Reserves).

    Output measure

    unit_measured_arcs(d) — the arcs that define a unit's capacity: the conversion_flow_coeff ≠ 0 output arcs of an indirect unit (the maxOutputSum arc set, _indirect_sum_arcs), the single capped arc of a direct unit (including the (p, src, p) arc of a sink-less unit). Inputs of indirect units are never in it.

    Rows

    name over row
    minFlow_minload_<kind> (p,d,t) ∈ p_idx, online ∩ min_load units Σ_A v_flow ≥ v_online·min_load + Rdn_p
    minFlow_output_floor_<kind> (p,p,k,d,t), units with ≥ 2 measured outputs, capacity_min_coeff_k > 0 v_flow ≥ v_online·min_load·cmin_k + Rdn_k
    minFlow_reserve (p,d,t), units with a reducing reserve on A, not covered above Σ_A v_flow ≥ Rdn_p
    minFlow_reserve_arc (p,s,k,d,t), reducing reserve on an arc of a multi-output unit or outside A v_flow ≥ Rdn_a
    minInputShare (p,s,p,d,t), indirect units with ≥ 2 conv≠0 inputs, share_s > 0 conv_s·in_s ≥ share_s·Σ conv·in

    Rdn = reserve that reduces the arc: down at the sink node, up at the source node. Factor 1 when v_flow is the flow at that node (sink side; an indirect unit's input; a sink-less unit); 1/slope for a source-side reserve on a direct input→output arc. All rows are dimensionless (unit-count flow units) in the autoscale registry.

    Decisions: the floor is not scaled by availability; connections are excluded; an indirect unit's input-side up reserve gets only the zero floor on that input arc (expressing "the input cut takes the outputs below min load" needs the conversion mix), so combined with output-side down reserve such a unit can end up below min_load.

    Upward reserve and online capacity

    Upward reserve held at a node a process delivers into (b.reserve_up_to_sink, b.reserve_up_sink_parts from core._reserve_coupling) sits on the LHS of the capacity rows, with coefficient 1 (v_flow and v_reserve are both in output units of unitsize):

    • built capacity, units: maxFlow (per arc) and maxOutputSum (sum of an indirect unit's outputs). On an indirect unit's INPUT arc maxFlow's M (core._max_flow_rhs) uses x(availability) in place of plain availability — the min-load section is drawn per unit online, not per available unit, credited only to a unit with an online variable (#102; same x as maxInputFlow_online below, computed once and shared);

    • online capacity, units with online variables (_online.py): maxFlow_online_<kind>: v_flow + Σ_r Rup ≤ v_online·avail·cap_max_coeff per capped arc, and maxOutputSum_online_<kind>: Σ_k v_flow + Σ_k Σ_r Rup ≤ v_online·avail.

    maxFlow / maxFlow_negCap / maxFlow_online_* read the units-only aggregate b.reserve_up_to_sink_units; a connection's reserve is limited by its own rows (connection_reserve_*, "Connections and reserve" below). profile_flow_upper_limit keeps the term for every process (b.reserve_up_to_sink). Hand-built data without process_unit keeps the process-generic coupling.

    So an offline unit holds no upward reserve (spinning reserve). The built-capacity rows keep their term for online processes too (redundant while online ≤ built; they carry the invest/divest terms). reserve_process_upward (max_share) still uses the built capacity. Together with the floors the online band is v_online·min_load + Rdn ≤ Σ out ≤ v_online·cap·avail − Rup.

    Source-side reserve and capacity

    The mirror of the floors: reserve that raises a unit arc's flow. _unit_floors.increasing_source_selectors(d, arcs) gives (down_src_flow, down_src_eff) keyed (p, r, ud, source) over all unit arcs (all_unit_arcs): down reserve at the source node of an arc whose v_flow is the input (sink == p: an indirect unit's input, a sink-less unit; φ = 1) or of a direct input→output arc (source ≠ p ≠ sink; φ = 1/slope, _inverse_slope). core._reserve_coupling turns them into b.reserve_down_at_source (per arc (p, source, sink, d, t), converted by φ) and b.reserve_down_input_arcs; the reducing up source selectors give b.reserve_up_at_source for profile_flow_lower_limit.

    row term
    maxFlow + Σ_r v_reserve_down[p,r,s]·φ; a bound input arc carrying the reserve gets a row too (the v_flow bound stays, redundant)
    maxFlow_online_<kind> same term against online capacity
    maxInputFlow_online_<kind> (new) an online indirect unit's input arc with the reserve: v_flow + Σ_r v_reserve_down ≤ v_online·x(avail)·p_indirect_input_cap, x(y) = (slope·W(y) + section)/(slope·W(1) + section) — the min-load section is drawn per unit online, so avail·p_indirect_input_cap would cap the derated full output (x = avail without a section)
    profile_flow_upper_limit / _lower_limit + down at the source / − up at the source, × φ
    reserve_process_{upward,downward} max_share · c, c = input capacity per unit at an input node (_reserve._input_capacity_factor: p_indirect_input_cap, slope + section, 1)

    Per-arc simplifications (as the floors): a direct unit with several outputs has one arc per output from the same input, and the reserve term joins each of them on (p, source), so the reserve needs headroom on every output arc (conservative; _input_capacity_factor de-duplicates (p, n)). An indirect unit's up reserve at an output (maxOutputSum) and down reserve at an input (maxFlow on the input arc) are the same "burn more" action but are checked in separate rows, so the headroom can be counted twice; a multi-input indirect unit is only kept within each input's own capacity. The reverse arc of a two-way unit (2way_2var) is a direct arc in process_source_sink_eff: down reserve at its node is headroom on the arc that draws from the node and a floor on the arc that feeds it. Connections have their own rows ("Connections and reserve" below).

    Reserve and ramp rows

    core._eq_ramp adds the reserve of an arc to its ramp rows, from the same _reserve_coupling aggregates as maxFlow and the floors (no new selector):

    row reserve term meaning
    ramp_{sink,source}_up_constraint + b.reserve_up_to_sink (up at the arc's sink) + every b.reserve_down_at_source entry (down at its source, ×φ) R↑: raises the arc's flow
    ramp_{sink,source}_down_constraint − b.reserve_down_to_sink − every b.reserve_up_at_source entry (×φ) R↓: lowers it

    Sink-side and source-side rows of a direct arc limit the same v_flow and both carry the terms. The rule is per arc, as the floors: upward reserve at an indirect unit's output does not load the ramp room of its input arcs. For a unit WITHOUT online variables (or a built-only row) the row is v_flow[t] − v_flow[t′] ± R ≤/≥ ±ρ·c·cap with ρ the room of the pair of steps (ramp_speed·60·(Δ′+Δ)/2) and cap = existing_count (±invest/divest); a unit ramping down from t′ to t can hold its decrease in addition as upward reserve (that is what "reserve reduces the ramp room" means per model step; activation-time-scaled room is item 81).

    For a unit WITH online variables (c14c part C, design §3) the room is on the units online, split by state, instead of on existing_count flat: the existing_count/invest/divest terms drop out of that row entirely and are replaced (per UC class linear/integer) by

    up:    + c·ρ̄·v_online + c·(SU − ρ̄)·v_startup − F[t′]·v_shutdown
down:  − c·ρ̄·v_online + (c·ρ̄ + F[t])·v_startup − c·SD′·v_shutdown

    (SU/SD′ as in "Startup / shutdown capacity reduction" below, this row's own side only — not the min over sides maxFlow_online keeps). F is the arc's OWN per-unit-online floor (_unit_floors.arc_floor_share, the same sets minFlow_minload / minFlow_output_floor use):

    arc F
    the only arc of the unit's output measure A_p (a direct unit's single arc, or a single-output indirect unit's output) min_load
    an output of a multi-output unit with capacity_min_coeff > 0 min_load · capacity_min_coeff
    any other (other outputs, input arcs, units without min_load) 0

    A single-input indirect unit's own fuel (input) arc also gets F = 0 here, by design, not oversight (c14c critique item 10): conversion_indirect plus the output floor already give input ≥ (slope·ml + section)·on = c·ml_in·on structurally, so F_in = c·ml_in would be a valid, tighter floor there too (closing the same pair-gain gap the output-side table closes). c14c's own target was single-output arcs only; the input-arc floor is left for a follow-up (see "Follow-ups" below, item 6), not adopted silently.

    So a continuing unit's online capacity changes by at most ρ̄ each step, a starter reaches at most SU (reusing maxFlow_online's startup-step limit) and a stopper floors at F at the predecessor step; downwards a stopper drops at most from SD′ and a starter rises at least to F. v_startup / v_shutdown are relative to the SAME online predecessor online_change uses, so a start/stop pair with online unchanged gains at most c·(ml − ½·ρ·Δ) − F ≤ 0 of room on a single-output arc (strictly negative: no free room from declaring a pair there); an input, or one output of a multi-output unit, can still gain up to the min-load share, paid by the startup cost (research_backbone_startup_ramp.md §1/§3).

    On a continuation roll's carry row (the period's first step ramping from the previous roll's realized flow, dt_ramp_carry) the predecessor is history, not this roll's data: the up row's stopper floor is F = 0 (no v_shutdown term at all there — the carried step's min_load isn't in this roll), and the down row's stopper allowance is SD′_carry = max(1, f̂ / (c·ĉ)) (f̂ = the carried flow, ĉ = p_roll_continue_online; 1 where ĉ = 0, no stoppers then) — exact and always feasible for any history the previous roll realized, unlike a fixed κ/c guess (critique item 3). Starters at the carry row keep SU/F as usual (this roll's own data).

    Steps without a predecessor (dt_ramp_link / dt_ramp_carry), where the arc holds reserve in that direction (prundt: at its sink in the same direction, at its source in the opposite one), get a reserve-only row R ≤ ρ·c·cap with ρ = ramp_speed·60·Δ (the linked row with v_flow[t′] = v_flow[t]); cap = v_online for a unit with online variables, existing_count otherwise. Rows inside a period with ρ ≥ 1 are dropped also with reserve: maxFlow* carries the same R↑ and the floors (minFlow_*) the same R↓. Declaring a startup or shutdown no longer buys extra ramp or reserve room by itself (the c14a flat + v_startup / − v_shutdown term is gone; Q-c14a-B is resolved by this split, not left open). Connections have no ramp parameters.

    Startup / shutdown capacity reduction

    _online._cap_reduction (Q8, item 49): for an online unit's arc with a ramp limit on a side (process_source_sink_ramp_limit_*, docs: "if the unit also has ramp limits"; GMPL keyed it on the speed alone), SU = min(1, ml + ½·ramp_speed_up·60·Δt) (the tighter side when both), red_su = max(0, availability − SU); SD / red_sd with the down speed at the pre-shutdown step. The half step is the step average of a unit synchronising at min load (owner question Q-c14a-A; GMPL used the full step). Availability correction: GMPL subtracted (1 − SU)·v_startup from availability·online, limiting started units to availability − 1 + SU; red_su gives them min(availability, SU) (identical at availability 1).

    Rows (maxFlow_online_<kind>, and maxInputFlow_online_<kind> on an indirect unit's input arc):

    row reduction
    base row κ·red_su·v_startup[t] + κ·max(0, red_sd − red_su)·v_shutdown[t⁺]
    …_shutdown_<kind> (where red_sd > 0 and t has a UNIQUE successor t⁺) κ·red_sd·v_shutdown[t⁺] + κ·max(0, red_su − red_sd)·v_startup[t]
    …_shutdown_branch_<kind> (one row PER successor, where t has SEVERAL successors — a branch point) κ·red_sd·v_shutdown[t⁺] + κ·max(0, red_su − red_sd)·v_startup[t]

    The pair is the exact aggregate bound for any mix of starts at t and stops at t⁺ (one-step runs get min(SU, SD)); GMPL's single row with both reductions is the minimum-uptime ≥ 2 form and forbids one-step runs. t⁺ ranges over every successor whose dt_ramp_link predecessor is t (c14c #98 / critique item 1): the same period (the original case), or the next period continuing the timeline (two periods on consecutive parts of one timeline, or a mid-horizon branch continuing from its trunk step) — none at a period's last step that does not continue, before a gap, a representative-block start or a roll's last step. At a branch point (several mid-horizon branches continuing from the same trunk step) the shutdown cross-term cannot be split across the single base row (one v_flow[t] variable, one row): the base row keeps only its startup term there (a valid, looser bound) and …_shutdown_branch_<kind> gives the tight bound per branch instead — summing the branches' v_shutdown into one row would let several mutually-exclusive branches each buy back a whole unit of ramp room from the shared trunk step. κ = capacity_max_coeff on capped arcs, p_indirect_input_cap on input arcs. ml: min_load on output-measured arcs (direct arcs: also for an input-side ramp, v_flow is the output); on an indirect unit's input arc the input drawn at min load per unit of input limit, x / input_share_max with x = (slope·W(min_load) + section)/(slope·W(1) + section) (W(y) the output fuel width filled to y, as p_indirect_input_cap), so a started unit can always draw its min-load input. A ramp-limited input arc of an online indirect unit gets maxInputFlow_online rows even without reserve. On input arcs availability is replaced by x(availability) in the row and in red = max(0, x(availability) − SU_in) (_online._input_availability_share). Per arc: maxOutputSum_online of a multi-output unit is not reduced (as GMPL; ramps are arc attributes).

    Reserve shortfall multiplier

    vq_reserve ∈ [0, 1] (bounded for numerics) is multiplied by reserve_shortfall_scale(d) in every reserveBalance_* row, in the objective penalty and in process_outputs (calc_slacks):

    • timeseries: reservation — returned as the very same Param when no dynamic / n-1 group exists (byte-identical LP);
    • dynamic: Σ p_flow_upper·unitsize·increase_reserve_ratio over the flows of the dynamic RHS (sink and source side);
    • n-1: max_f Σ p_flow_upper·unitsize·large_failure_ratio, the sum over the failing flows of one process f, maximised over the failing processes (_flow_requirement_bound(per_process_max=True)): the largest single n-1 row;
    • source side (a flow out of the group's node, dynamic and n-1 down): the bound of an arc in process_source_sink_eff is multiplied by the full-load draw slope + section (_with_full_load_draw; section only for min_load_efficiency units with online variables), because the rows count the flow drawn from the node as nodeBalance_eq does (_reserve._drawn_terms: v_flow·unitsize·slope + v_online·section·unitsize on an efficiency arc, v_flow·unitsize elsewhere); v_online ≤ p_flow_upper makes it a valid bound;
    • dynamic group demand: group_demand_requirement(d) = ratio_g · Σ_{n∈g} max(−inflow, 0) / Δt (exact, a constant), the same Param as the RHS term of reserveBalance_dynamic_eq. The inflow is p_negative_inflow_unmargined: the energy-margin emitters write solve_data/pdtNodeInflow_unmargined (the inflow before the margin) whenever a solve applies a margin, and keep it equal to pdtNodeInflow in later solves sharing the Provider; without the key the field is p_negative_inflow. Benders: the field is not region-filtered (like penalty_reserve); a subproblem joins it with its filtered group_node, so each region gets its own nodes' demand;
    • authored reservation is added for any group; methods add up.

    p_flow_upper is the structural bound (existing + max invest by that period, per unit size); unlimited invest and conversion_flow_coeff = 0 arcs carry max_flow_for_unconstrained_variables, so the multiplier stays finite. Rows with a zero multiplier are dropped (no coefficient).

    Activation (_reserve_methods.demanded_reserves, shared by _derived.prundt_from_source and emit/_reserve_sets): a reserve's relationships are active when the (reserve, up/down) has a requirement — a non-zero reservation on some group, a dynamic group driven by an increase_reserve_ratio at one of its nodes (or by its own group ratio), or an n-1 group with a large_failure_ratio at one of its nodes: dynamic and n-1 requirements are reserve demand by themselves. A dynamic / n-1 group without a driving ratio stays inactive. An n-1 ratio on a process without a flow in the failing direction activates the reserve with no n-1 row (inert columns).

    n-1 rows

    reserveBalance_{up,down}_n_1_eq[r, ud, g, p_fail, d, t]: one row per failing process p_fail (a process with large_failure_ratio at a node of g and a flow into, for up, or out of, for down, that node). LHS: v_reserve_total[r, ud, g, d, t] minus the reserve of p_fail at every node of g (× unitsize × reliability) plus the group's shared vq_reserve · scale; RHS: p_fail's failing flows × unitsize × ratio. The shared slack prices the largest deficit once (.mod parity).

    v_reserve_total (POWER column, no cost) exists for every group with an n-1 row and at least one provider; reserve_total_upper bounds it by the reserve of all providers: v_reserve_total − reserve_sum ≤ 0. Eliminating the total gives back the per-provider form (each n-1 row counts every provider except p_fail), so the reformulation is an exact projection. The inequality suffices because the total enters only ≥ requirement rows, with a positive sign, and has no cost. It needs reserve_sum ≥ 0, which holds because providers have reliability > 0 (process_reserve_upDown_node_active) and unitsize > 0; otherwise v_reserve_total ≥ 0 would add a constraint. A group without providers (or without reliability data) has no total: its rows are vq_reserve · scale ≥ failure.

    The reserveBalance_timeseries_eq / reserveBalance_dynamic_eq rows of a group that has a total use v_reserve_total in place of reserve_sum (exact by the same projection; groups without n-1 rows keep reserve_sum). This roughly halves the reserve block of groups combining n-1 with another method. The value function in the timeseries right-hand side is unchanged, so the reported reserve price (the timeseries row duals) is the same wherever it is unique.

    Nonzeros per (r, ud, g, d, t), with P provider pairs (p, n), and per failing process f its own provider pairs own_f and failing arcs a_f: (P + 1) + Σ_f (2 + own_f + a_f) (the per-provider form had Σ_f (P − own_f + 1 + a_f)), so they grow with providers plus failing processes. HiGHS presolve keeps the total columns (measured with HiGHS 1.14: presolve neither removes the total columns nor re-densifies the rows).

    Results: the objective and every value that is unique at the optimum are those of the per-provider form; v_reserve is costless, so its split among providers is often not unique and may differ between the two forms. The n-1 row duals keep their meaning; when the total is positive, the magnitude of the reserve_total_upper dual is the sum of the duals of the group-step's rows that use the total (HiGHS reports the ≤ row with the opposite sign to the ≥ rows). The total is not written to the outputs.

    Connections and reserve

    _reserve._add_connection_reserve_constraints. Connections are the processes of process_source_sink that are not in process_unit (_common._connection_procs; process_unit None — hand-built data — keeps the old process-generic coupling; an empty process_unit makes every process a connection, so the DB projection keeps it empty instead of None). maxFlow, maxFlow_negCap and maxFlow_online_* carry the upward reserve of units only (b.reserve_up_to_sink_units).

    At a reserve node n a connection p has at most one in-channel (a flow variable arriving at n: v_flow[p,m,n], or v_flow_back[p,n,m] of a one-variable / DC arc) and at most one out-channel (v_flow[p,n,m] drawing slope·v_flow on an efficiency arc, or v_flow_back[p,m,n]); more raises. Flows are measured where they arrive; s_out is the power drawn at n per unit of the out-channel. Net-position rule:

    R↑_n ≤ (C_in − x_in) + s_out·x_out        R↓_n ≤ x_in + s_out·(C_out − x_out)
up:   x_in − s_out·x_out + R↑ ≤ C_in                  (connection_reserve_up)
down: x_out − x_in/s_out + R↓/s_out ≤ C_out           (connection_reserve_down)

    One row per capacity bound of the channel whose capacity the reserve uses (in-channel for up, out-channel for down): built (C and the invest / divest terms of that arc's maxFlow / maxFlow_back), _online_{linear,integer} (κ·availability·v_online), _exact (Ū·y on the forward arc, Ū·(1−y) on the reverse arc), so each row holds a single channel's capacity and is exact. A missing channel contributes nothing (C = 0, s_out = 1): a one-way connection gets the floor R↑ ≤ s·x_out at its sending node and x + R↑ ≤ C (the old maxFlow coupling) at its receiving node; a one-variable connection gets R↑ ≤ C − f, R↓ ≤ C + f for its signed flow f into n; reserve at a node the connection does not link is 0. The same capacity may back upward reserve at one end and downward reserve at the other (one action serves both), as for units (separate up / down rows).

    Kept as they were: reserve_process_* (max_share × built capacity, c = 1 for connections) and profile_flow_upper_limit, which keeps the upward-reserve term on the in-arc of a connection with a connection__profile — conservative: it does not count reserve from stopping the out-flow.

    The reverse flow v_flow_back also counts in the dynamic and n-1 requirements, their shortfall scale and the storage energy rows (reverse orientation of a one-variable connection to a storage node, slope 1), with the orientation of nodeBalance_eq (into the arc's source, out of its sink).
